Understand the Metrics

Numbers With Context

NOI

Net Operating Income

Effective property income after modeled vacancy and operating expenses, before financing costs.

CAP

Cap Rate

NOI divided by purchase price. Commonly used to compare income-producing commercial properties.

CoC

Cash-on-Cash

Modeled annual cash flow divided by the initial cash invested in the transaction.

DSCR

Debt Coverage

NOI divided by modeled annual debt service. It indicates how much income covers the debt payment.

The Commercial Investment Blueprint™

What Smart Investors Verify Before They Buy

This is not a motivational checklist. It is the decision framework behind a serious commercial real estate review: what is producing the income, what can break it, how the debt changes the return, and what price still makes sense when the assumptions get worse.

01

Define the Investment Thesis

Know the goal before evaluating the property: target cash yield, appreciation strategy, hold period, acceptable leverage, liquidity needs and planned exit.

  • Target cash-on-cash return
  • Target hold period
  • Value-add vs. stabilized strategy
  • Walk-away return threshold
02

Audit the Income — Not Just the Asking Cap Rate

Study the quality and durability of the income. A cap rate means very little if the rent roll or lease expirations are weak.

  • Rent roll and actual collections
  • Lease expirations and rollover concentration
  • Tenant credit and concentration risk
  • Rent bumps, options and reimbursements
03

Normalize the NOI

Rebuild NOI from operating reality instead of accepting the seller's pro forma. Separate recurring expenses from one-time items and add costs the property will actually require.

  • Taxes and insurance after acquisition
  • Management fees
  • Repairs, reserves and recurring CapEx
  • Vacancy, concessions and bad debt
04

Underwrite the Debt

The same property can be attractive or dangerous depending on the financing. Model the debt before deciding what the equity return really is.

  • LTV / LTC and equity required
  • DSCR and debt yield
  • Interest rate and amortization
  • IO period, recourse, covenants and prepayment
05

Verify the Market & Submarket

Compare the property against the actual competitive set—not a broad city average.

  • Comparable rents and recent sales
  • Vacancy and absorption
  • New supply / development pipeline
  • Employment, traffic, anchors and demand drivers
06

Stress-Test the Deal

A base case tells you what happens if you are right. A stress test tells you whether you can survive being wrong.

  • Vacancy increases 5–10%
  • Expenses rise 10%
  • Interest rate moves higher
  • Exit cap expands 50–100 bps
07

Complete Real Due Diligence

The financial model is only one layer. Verify the legal, physical and operational facts before your contingencies expire.

  • Title, survey, zoning and permits
  • Environmental and property condition
  • Leases, estoppels and service contracts
  • Historical financials, taxes and insurance
08

Know Your Maximum Price

Turn the underwriting into a decision: the price that meets your return requirement, the contingencies you need, and the number where you walk away.

  • Base-case value
  • Downside-case value
  • Maximum offer / walk-away price
  • LOI and diligence protections
THREE NUMBERS THAT CAN LIE BY THEMSELVES

Cap Rate. Cash-on-Cash. Projected ROI.

Each can look impressive while hiding tenant rollover, deferred maintenance, unrealistic rents, underwritten expenses or risky leverage. The blueprint forces the numbers back into context.
